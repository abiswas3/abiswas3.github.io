    Lower Bound

    By Ari 01 February 2025
    • proof-complexity
    • combinatorics

    Contents

    These notes are not thoroughly reviewed. There maybe be typos (minor and major). But the main theorems hold.

    This post is about Refuting The Basic Pigeon Hole Principle (PHP) In The Resolution Proof System. For this post, the number of pigeons will be $m:=n$ and the number of holes will be $n-1$, and we only consider fomulae that fail to satisfy the Pigeon and Hole Axioms (so we are not considering the functional and onto axioms). See the crash course for a refresher on the resolution proof system, and the axioms of the PHP principle.

    $\PHPBasic$ refers to the CNF formulae that tries $n$ pigeons fit into $n-1$ holes, when the underlying graph is the complete bipartite graph, and we will show that all resolution proofs that show that this formula is unsatisfiable are long. Recall that the size of a resolution refutation is the number of clauses in the refutation.

    The original result is by Haken (1985)

    but we go over the proof by Beame & Pitassi (1996)

    which is a simplification of the original proof.

    : Proof Size Lower Bound

    Any refutation $\pi$ for $\PHPBasic$ in the resolution proof system has size $\BigOmega{\exp{n}}$.

    Preliminary Material#

    Sometimes it is easier to deal with formulae with no negated variables. For PHP refutations, as is standard in the literature (see [Razborov, 2003]

    , [Beame & Pitassi, 1996]

    ) we will often do the following conversion to obtain clause $\CPlus$ from a clause $C$. Reminder that $[n]$ is the index set of the pigeons, and $[n-1]$ is the index set for holes. The intended meaning of $x_{ij}=1$ is that pigeon $i$ is assigned to hole $j$ in the PHP formula.

    : Positive Conversion

    Let $\pi$ denote a refutation for $\PHPBasic$, whose variables are $x_{ij}$ for $i \in [m]$ and $j \in [n]$. Define $$ X_{ij} := \bigvee_{i’ \in [m]\setminus{i}} x_{i’j}. $$

    For any clause $C \in \pi$, we denote by $\CPlus$ the positive clause obtained from $C$ by replacing every negated literal $\Complement{x_{ij}}$ with the disjunction $X_{ij}$, leaving every positive literal unchanged.

    $X_{ij}=1$ implies there is at least one pigeon that is not $i$ that is assigned to hole $j$. Observe that for any assignment $\vec{x}$ satisfying the hole axioms[1], $(x_{ij} = 1) \implies X_{ij} = 0$.

    Finally we will call an assignment $\criticalAlpha{}$ which maps pigeons to holes, critical, if it matches every hole with a unique pigeon, and every pigeon but one to a unique hole.

    : Critical Assignments

    An assignment $\criticalAlpha{i} \in \bit^{n\times(n-1)}$ is $i$-critical if every pigeon except $i$ gets assigned a unique hole and pigeon $i$ remains unassigned.

    The first thing to note is that any critical assignment satisfies all hole axioms. The above also implies every hole gets assigned a unique pigeon. In the figure below the pigeons are $\{1, 2, 3\}$ and the set of holes are $\{1, 2\}$. The assignment is 2-critical because pigeon two is left unassigned, and the other pigeons get a unique hole.

    Critical assignments have the same behaviour on both the converted clause and the original clause.

    :

    Let $\pi$ be a $\PHPBasic$ refutation in the resolution proof system. For any clause $C \in \pi$, let $\CPlus$ denote its positive version. Then for any critical assignment $\criticalAlpha{} \in \bit^{n\times (n-1)}$, we have $C(\criticalAlpha{}) = \CPlus(\criticalAlpha{})$.

    Proof

    Say $\criticalAlpha{}$ sets $x_{ij} = 1$ for arbitrary literal in clause $C$. Then hole $j$ is already matched to pigeon $i$, and no other pigeon is mapped to $j$ (from the definition of a critical assignment). Therefore, $X_{ij} = \underset{i’\neq i}{\lor} x_{i’j} = 0 = \Complement{x_{ij}}$.

    Now, say $\criticalAlpha{}$ sets $x_{ij} = 0$ for arbitrary literal in clause $C$ i.e. $\Complement{x_{ij}} = 1$ Either $\alpha$ is $i$-critical or its $k$ critical for some $k \neq i$. Either ways, by criticality all the holes are matched to unique pigeons. As a result hole $j$ gets a pigeon $i’$ that is not $i$ nor $k$. Therefore, $X_{ij} = 1 = \Complement{x_{ij}}$ as $x_{i’j} = 1$.

    □
    : Width of a clause

    The width of a clause $C$, denoted with $w(C)$, is the number of literals in the clause.

    Before going into the details of the proof, we describe the general proof strategy:

    Show that for all valid refutations, a small proof length/size implies that the positive conversion of every clause in the proof has a small width (number of literals in clause). However, we show that a valid PHP refutation MUST have large width. Thus, we can conclude that the size of the proof must be large.

    Valid Refutations Have Wide Positive Clauses#

    : Resolution Refutations Have Large Width

    Every refutation $\pi$ of $\PHPBasic$ must contain a clause $C \in \pi$ with $w(\CPlus) \geq \frac{n^2}{9}$

    Proof

    For any clause $C’$ let $$\mathcal{P}(C’) := \{ i \in [n]: \exists \criticalAlpha{i} \in \bit^{n\times (n-1)} \text{ s.t } C’(\criticalAlpha{i}) = 0\}$$

    Let $\mu(C) = \Size{\mathcal{P}(C)}$. Then for each pigeon axiom $P_i$, by definition $\mu(P_i) = 1$.[1] On the other hand, $\mu(\emptyset) = [n]$ (as all assignments, and therefore all critical assignments falsify $\emptyset$ by definition).

    For any resolvent $C’$ that is derived from $A’$ and $B’$, we have $\mathcal{P}(C’) \subseteq \mathcal{P}(A’) \cup \mathcal{P}(B’)$, as $C’$ must have the combined literals in $A’$ and $B’$. Therefore, $\mu(C’) \leq \mu(A’) + \mu(B’)$.

    Set $\epsilon:=1/3$. Now walk the proof tree starting at the pigeon axioms $P_i$’s to the conclusion $\emptyset$, there must be some clause in the proof, lets call it $\CStar$, such that[2] its parents $A$ and $B$ have $\mu(A) < \epsilon n$ and $\mu(B) < \epsilon n$, but $C \geq \epsilon n$. This has to happen for us to get $\mu(\emptyset) = n$ as the resolvents critical pigeon set is upper bounded by the sum of its parents, and the first set of parents start at 1. Then we have

    $$\epsilon n \le \mu(\CStar) < 2\epsilon n \tag{a}$$

    Now pick arbitrary $i \in \mathcal{P}(\CStar)$ and arbitrary $j \notin \mathcal{P}(\CStar)$. There are $s:=\Size{\mu(\CStar)}$ choices for $i$ and $n-s$ choices for $j$. Let $\criticalAlpha{i}$ denote the critical assignment which causes $i$ to be in $P(\CStar)$. We construct $\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}$ by swapping row $i$ and $j$ in $\criticalAlpha{i} \in \bit^{n \times (n-1)}$.

    $\CStar(\criticalAlpha{i}) = 0$, and as $\criticalAlpha{i}$ is critical, by the lemma above we have $\CPlus(\criticalAlpha{i}) = 0$. We also have $\CStar(\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}) = 1$.[3] Similarly, $\CPlus(\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}) = 1$ by the same lemma.

    Say in $\criticalAlpha{i}$ pigeon $j$ was assigned hole $k$. Then in $\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}$, pigeon $i$ is assigned $k$ and $j$ is unassigned. Note, in $\criticalAlpha{i}$, we have $x_{ik}=0, x_{jk} =1$, and $\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}$ we have $x_{ik}=1, x_{jk}=0$. There is no other difference between the two critical assignments.

    Setting any variable to false should never flip a positive clause $\CPlus$ from false to true. Therefore setting $x_{jk}=0$ cannot make $\CPlus(\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}) = 1$. So the only way $\CPlus(\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j})$ is flipped to true is because pigeon $i$ got assigned a hole $k$. As there are no negated variables in positive conversions we can conclude that $x_{ik}$ must be in $\CPlus$.

    Thus, for a fixed $i$ and $j$ we got a unique hole $k$ such that $x_{ik}$ is in $\CPlus$. There are $s$ $i$‘s, and if we picked a different pigeon $j’$ to swap with $i$ we’d get a different hole $k’:= j’(k’)$, giving us a new literal $x_{ik’} \in \CPlus$. So we get a unique $x_{i, j(k)}$ literal in $\CPlus$ for each $j \notin \mathcal{P}(C)$. Therefore, we can conclude that $\CPlus$ has $s(n-s)$ literals.

    $$ w(\CPlus) := s(n-s) \geq \epsilon n (n - 2\epsilon n) = n^2/9 $$

    Where the last inequality comes from Equation $(a)$

    1. Any critical assignment that is not $i$-critical will satisfy $P_i$. Thus, the only critical assignments for which $P_i$ is not satisfied are the ones that screws over pigeon $i$. ↩

    2. Safe to assume $n$ is large enough such that $\epsilon n > 1$. ↩

    3. Assume not, then this implies $j \in P(\CStar)$ as $\criticalAlpha{i \leftrightarrow j}$ is critical, which contradicts our assumption that $j \notin \mathcal{P}(\CStar)$. ↩

    □

    Small Sized Proofs Imply Small Width Proofs#

    : Wide Clause

    Given $\epsilon \in (0,1)$ and a refutation $\pi$ for $\PHPBasic$, we say a clause $C \in \pi$ is $\epsilon$-wide if $w(\CPlus) \ge \epsilon n^2$

    Let $\pi$ be a refutation of $\PHPBasic$, and $\pi^{+} = (\CPlus)_{C \in \pi}$ be the refutation obtained by replacing every clause in $C \in \pi$ with its positive conversion.

    Let $S_0$ be the set of $\epsilon$-wide clauses in $\pi^+$ and $s_0 := \Size{S_0}$. Wideness implies that each clauses $S \in S_0$ has at least $\epsilon$ fraction of the possible $n(n-1)$ literals (which can be upper bounded by $n(n+1) < n^2)$. Therefore by the averaging argument, there must be one literal, let’s call it $x_{ij}$, that is present[2] in at least $\epsilon s_0$ of all the clauses in $S_0$

    Labelling Strategy:

    • We set $x_{ij} = 1$ and $x_{i’j} = 0$ for all $i’ \neq i$: We have assigned pigeon $i$ the hole $j$, and no other pigeon $i’$ is assigned $j$. In other words, we have satisfied pigeon axiom $P_i$ and hole axiom $H_j^{i,i’}$ for all pairs $i\neq i’$.

    • We also set $x_{ij’} = 0$ for all $j’ \neq j$: This ensures $i$ is not matched to any hole $j’$ that is not $j$. As a result we can ignore pigeon $i$ and hole $j$, and consider them sorted.

    Setting $x_{ij} = 1$ implies we satisfy at least $\epsilon s_0$ of the clauses in $S_0$ (as these clauses have no negations, and $x_{ij}$ was the literal that was present in all these clauses from the averaging argument). Thus, the number of wide and unsatisfied clauses left in $S_0$ is at most $s_0(1 - \epsilon)$.

    Also the way we picked our labelling strategy[3], if $\CPlus \in \pi^+$ is satisfied, then so is the corresponding $C \in \pi$ it was created from. Define $\pi_1 \subseteq \pi$ by applying the above labelling strategy to every clause $C \in \pi$, and getting ride of the satisfied clauses, which gives us a valid refutation of $\PHP{n-1}{n-2}{K}$.

    Now we repeat the same process $k=\lceil\frac{\log S_0}{\epsilon}\rceil$ times. Each time we will find a new literal $x_{ij}$ that is present in $\epsilon$ fraction of the wide clauses remaining in $S_0$. Then we re-apply our labelling strategy, shrinking the size of $S_0$ to $s_0(1-\epsilon)^k$. By assigning variables based on the labelling strategy, we are left with $\pi_k$ which is a refutation of $\PHP{n-k}{n-k -1}{K}$.

    We claim that after $k$ rounds of the above process, there should be no wide clauses left in $\pi_k$. To see why

    \begin{align*} \text{Number of wide clauses left} &\le (1 - \epsilon)^k S_0 \le e^{-k\epsilon}S_0 < 1 \end{align*}

    Where the last inequality comes from plugging in the value of $k$, and the second last inequality comes from Taylors theorem. From the lemma above we have that $\exists \CStar \in \pi_k$ such that $w(\CPlus) \ge (n-k)^2/9$, if $\pi_k$ as a valid refutation. However, from the above no more wide clauses left we have also that $\CPlus$ cannot be $\epsilon$-wide.

    Combining the two we get, that

    $$ \frac{(n-k)^2}{9} = \frac{\left(n-\frac{\log S_0}{\epsilon}\right)^2}{9} \le w(\CStar^+) < \epsilon n^2$$

    As $\epsilon$ is some small constant, and solving for $S_0$ we get $S_0 \ge \exp(\BigOmega{n})$. And as $S_0 \le \Size{\pi}$, we get a lower bound on the size of any refutation.
