Impact On Jolt: Faster Binding

Jolt experiences 1.6x speedup in polynomial binding as a result of optimised Montgomery multiplication, and cache-aware parallelisation. Furthermore, owing to better caching, and better parallelistion, we sped up multi-linear binding by nearly 1.8-2x. We elaborate more on this below.

Problem Statement

Experiments described in this post revealed to us that speedups from better Montgomery multiplication, did not manifest in end to end traces.

There was however, clear and definitive evidence in the micro-benches for multiplication, (and binding1), that our multiplication algorithm was indeed faster.
In an extremely odd2 turn of events, turning all the binding algorithms to being sequential brought back speedup (to an extent). We say odd because logically, the only difference is between the optimised version, and the base version, is that the now rayon threads must execute fewer multiplication operations. This would imply that every thread should be doing lesser work, and therefore parallelisation would have nothing to do with our optimisation algorithm. The issue is that the real bottleneck in our computation – is bringing the actual coefficients of the polynomial from DRAM into caches (and not doing fewer multiplications).

Why Does Large Poly Binding not work

Here’s another phenemenon that looks weird at first, but once we think about caching it begins to make sense. The gains from our optimisation seem to be going away as we increase the number of variables in the multi-variate polynomial. The answer to this, once again is simply – caching. Initially, when the polynomials have fewer variables – all the coefficients of the polynomial are in cache. As the polynomials grow larger, we need to move coefficients from DRAM into the cache. As cited above, this process takes about 150-200 cycles.
