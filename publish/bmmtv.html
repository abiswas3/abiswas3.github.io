Hyper BMMTV Costs

The purpose of this post is to understand the computational costs of committing to an \(\ell\) variate multi-linear polynomial using the inner product commitment scheme prescribed by (Bünz et al., 2019).

Note that (Bünz et al., 2019) only describes how to commit to a univariate polynomial or a bi-variate polynomial explicitly.
To commit to a multi-linear polynomial, we feed the univariate polynomial scheme as a blackbox to the reduction of knowledge technique described in Section 6 of (Zhao et al., 2024) (titled hyper-kzg).

Notation

Throughout this post we assume that use \(m \in \mathbb{N}\) is a power of 2. When describing group operations, we stick with the conventions used in CITE, and use multiplicative notation.

A note on notation: This area is ripe with the use of non-standard notation. Sometimes, this is unavoidable to succinctly describe protocols, which can otherwise be quite difficult to describe. Often however, we feel this is unnecessary. So we will do our best to describe everything using standard notation accepted by the wider mathematics community, outside of crypto and snarks. Additionally, we will avoid much of the formalism typically found in the preliminary sections of the papers cited above. We believe this approach simplifies the exposition significantly without compromising rigour or correctness.

Background

To fully make sense of the commitment scheme, we need to understand 4 sub-components

  1. The KZG commitment scheme: You can ignore the section on batching, as that’s not critical for understanding inner product arguments.
  2. MIPP-k: We dive into the details in this post. Given commitments \(T \in \mathbb{G}_T\) and \(U \in \mathbb{G}_1\), a public vector \(\vec{b} \in \mathbb{F}^m\), the goal is to prove that the \(U = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} A_i^{b_i}\) where \((A_0, \ldots A_{m-1})\) is the message committed to by \(T\).
  3. Using the above two schemes to commit to a bivariate polynomial, and then how to use that bivariate scheme to commit to a univariate polynomial.
  4. How to commit to multi-linear polynomials using univariate commitments

As the goal is to understand performance, we ignore soundness/knowledge soundness proofs, and take proofs cited in the above papers as gospel. We do re-prove completeness most of the time

Research Question: Instead of this malarkey of going from bi-variate polynomial to a univariate polynomial, and then using that univariate polynomial commitment scheme as a blackbox, is there a more direct way to commit to multi-variate polynomials – using inner product commitments.

Inner Product Commitments From Doubly Homomorphic Commitments

The original paper describes this section generally to handle a variety of inner product maps using an abstract but general protocol. In this post, as our focus is on performance, we will abandon any abstractions, and describe everything concretely.

The Task At Hand

The prover is given witness \(\vec{a} = (a_0, \ldots, a_{m-1}) \in \mathbb{G}_1^m\), and commitment key \(\vec{v} = (v_1, \ldots, v_m) \in \mathbb{G}_2^m\). To commit to \(\vec{a}\), it computes \(C_{\vec{a}}=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1} e(a_i, v_i) \in \mathbb{G}_T\). This commitment scheme is due to CITE.

What We Want To Verify: The verifier is given \(C_{\vec{a}} \in \mathbb{G}_T\), a public vector \(\vec{b}^m \in \mathbb{F}^m\) and claimed value \(u = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} a_i^{b_i} \in \mathbb{G}_1\), and they want to verify that the \(u\) is indeed as claimed, and \(a_0, \dots, a_{m-1}\) is the opening of \(C_{\vec{a}}\).

The rest of this section is devoted to understanding how this verification process works, and quantifying the computational complexity of proving the above statement.

The Protocol

The prover has \(\vec{a}, \vec{v}, \mathtt{pk}\)

The verifier has \(\vec{b} , \mathtt{vk}\)

Bi-variate Polynomials

We are given a bi-variate polynomial \(f \in \mathbb{F}[X, Y]\) \[f(X, Y) = \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} f_i(Y)X^{i}\] where for all \(i \in \{0, \ldots, m-1\}\), \(f_i \in \mathbb{F}[Y]\) is a univariate polynomial with degree at most \(\ell -1\) i.e \(f_i(y) = f_{(i, 0)}y^0 + \ldots + f_{(i, \ell-1)}y^{\ell-1}\) where \(f_{i,j} \in \mathbb{F}\) for all \(j \in \{0, \ldots, \ell-1\}\)

Setup

\(g\) is a public generator for \(\mathbb{G}_1\) and \(h\) is a public generator for \(\mathbb{G}_2\), and \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{F}\) are the random secret values sampled by trusted setup.

The prover receives \[ \mathtt{pk}:= \left\{ \begin{aligned} &\vec{w} = [w_j]_{j=0}^{\ell-1} = \left[ g^{\alpha^i} \right]_{i=0}^{\ell - 1}, \\ &\vec{v} = [v_i]_{i=0}^{2m-2} = \left[ h^{\beta^{2i}} \right]_{i=0}^{2m - 2}, \\ &\hat{h} \gets \mathbb{G}_2 \end{aligned} \right\} \]

How To Commit

For all \(i \in \{0, \ldots, m-1\}\) we commit to each univariate polynomial \(f_{i} \in \mathbb{F}[Y]\) using the KZG commitment scheme with commitment key \(\vec{w} \in \mathbb{G}_1^m\). Let \(A_i \in \mathbb{G}_1\) denote this commitment. i.e.

\[ \begin{align} A_i &= \prod_{j=0}^{\ell -1} w_j^{f_{(i,j)}} = \prod_{j=0}^{\ell -1} \Big(g^{\alpha^j}\Big)^{f_{(i,j)}} = g^{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}f_{i,j}\alpha^j} \end{align} \] COST: These requires \(\ell\) \(\mathbb{G}_1\) exponentiations and \(\ell\) \(\mathbb{G}_1\) multiplications.

Then we compute the inner pairing commitment under \(\vec{v}\) using the KZG commitments as witness. The final commitment \(T \in \mathbb{G}_T\) \[T = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} e(A_j, v_j)\] COST: \(m\) pairing operations

Prover \(\rightarrow\) Verifier: \(T \in \mathbb{G}_T\) as the commitment to \(f \in \mathbb{F}[X, Y]\)

How To Open

The verifier asks the prover to open \(f\) at points \((z_x, z_y) \in \mathbb{F}\times \mathbb{F}\). As a response the prover

  1. Gives a partial evaluation \(U = f(z_x, Y) = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1} A_i^{(z_x)^i}\). Note that \(U\) is a KZG commitment to the univariate polynomial \(f(z_x, Y) = \sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1} \Big(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1} f_{i, j}(z_x)^i\Big)Y^j\) (see here for the derivation)
  2. The prover provides a proof \(\pi_1\) that \(U\) is the inner product of the opening of \(T\) and the vector \(\vec{z}_x = 1, \ldots, (z_x)^{m-1}\)
  3. The prover provides a KZG proof \(\pi_2\) that \(\nu\) is the evaluation of \(f(z_x, y)\) at \(z_y\) and \(U\) is the commitment to univariate polynomial \(f(z_x, y)\)

In summary, the prover sends to the verifier: \(U \in \mathbb{G}_1\), \(\nu \in \mathbb{F}\), proofs \(\pi_1\) and \(\pi_2\)

To open the prover must provide two proofs

  • \(\pi_1\) this is a proof that \(T\) is an inner product commitment with witness \((A_{0}, \ldots, A_{m-1})\) and \((v_0, \ldots, v_{m-1})\).
  • \(\pi_2\) is a KZG opening proof that \(U\) is a polynomial commitment to \(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1} f_i(Y)X^{i}\) and \(\nu=f(z_x, z_y)\)

How To Verify

To verify

  1. Check if \(U\) is an inner product of the opening of \(T\) and the public vector \(\vec{z_x} =( 1, \ldots, (z_x)^{m-1}\). This is done by the following the steps described in CITE.

  2. Next open a KZG commitment.

If both are valid accept.

Inner Product Openings

The verifier is given

  • \(\mathtt{vk}\)
  • \(U \in \mathbb{G}_1\)
  • \(T \in \mathbb{G}_T\)
  • A vector \(\vec{b}=(b_0, \ldots, b_{m-1}) \in \mathbb{F}^{m}\)

told that \(U = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1}A_i^{b_i}\) and \(T = \prod_{i=0}^{m-1}e(A_i, v_i)\) where \(v_i\) is as defined in the setup.

where \(\vec{A} = (A_0, \ldots, A_{m-1}) \in \mathbb{G}_1^m\) is the witness known by the prover.
